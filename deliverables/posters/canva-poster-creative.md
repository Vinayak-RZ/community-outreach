Design an A3 landscape poster (420 × 297 mm, 300 dpi), "Campus dogs: two sides of one street", for EEC 399 Community Outreach, Group 23, IIT Roorkee. Make it a bright, friendly, illustrated infographic that stops people walking past: a big central illustration with the two sides branching out to its left and right, each point with its own small round illustration, like a health-tips infographic. Fun to look at, but every word and number below is real research, so keep it readable from 1.5 m.

Copy rules:
- The six quotes on the two sides are final. Print them exactly as written here, word for word, with their spelling, capitals and punctuation. Do not correct, shorten or restyle their wording.
- Everything else in this prompt is draft copy. You may shorten it, reword it or split it into shorter lines so it fits the layout and reads well on a poster. Keep every number, fraction, date, name and fact as it is, and keep the meaning of each line.
- Do not add facts, numbers, quotes, names or sections that are not in this prompt.

## Look

- Background pale sky blue #EAF4FB, with scattered small light-blue plus signs and a few dotted coral circles (#F06B6B) as decoration.
- Flat vector illustration style throughout: simple shapes, soft outlines, no gradients, no photos. People and dogs drawn in the same friendly flat style, dogs as ordinary Indian street dogs (tan, brown, black and white), not cartoon breeds.
- Two side colours: terracotta #C2502E for Against, deep teal #1F6F6B for For. Accent mustard #E8B03A for numbers and highlights. Text charcoal #22303C.
- Title set in stacked blocks, like the reference infographic: dark words on the background, and the key phrase in white on a solid coloured box.
- Type: a rounded bold sans (Poppins or Nunito) for headings and numbers, Inter for body. Body 12–14 pt, source labels 9 pt grey.
- No emoji, no team photos. British spelling.

## Layout (landscape)

**Top 60% of the sheet: the hook.**

- Centre top, the title in blocks:
  - Small line: EEC 399 Community Outreach · Group 23 · Topic 3 · Dept. of Electrical Engineering, IIT Roorkee · Autumn 2026
  - "CAMPUS DOGS" in large charcoal caps.
  - "TWO SIDES OF ONE STREET" in white on a deep teal box, with a terracotta box behind it offset a few mm, so the block itself has two sides.
- Centre, under the title: the hero illustration. A student and a campus dog standing on a campus road, facing each other at a respectful distance, in front of a large round badge split down the middle, left half terracotta, right half teal. Two or three dotted coral circles float around them.
- Under the illustration, centred, 16 pt: Most people we asked would keep the dogs. The fight is about where they are allowed to be.
- Left of centre: heading AGAINST in terracotta, then three items. Each item is a small round illustration on the outer side, a bold quote, and a grey source label.
- Right of centre: heading FOR in teal, three items the same way, mirrored.
- Two round stat bubbles on each side, sitting between the quotes and the hero illustration, mustard number large, label small.

**Bottom 40%: three cards on white rounded panels, then a timeline ribbon.**

- Left card: what we found, and where sources disagree.
- Centre card (widest): the map.
- Right card: what we did, proposed, and the KPIs.
- Ribbon across the bottom: the fieldwork timeline as a dotted road with 17 milestone pins.
- Footer line under the ribbon.

### Left card: What we found

Two mini columns with small coloured headings.

"Why the Against side is angry" (terracotta):
- The hardest lines come from people who were chased, mostly at night, on a few roads.
- Dogs inside hostels, in washrooms and on beds, is the most common complaint.
- Mess, canteen and cleaning staff were the most negative group we spoke to.

"Why the For side holds firm" (teal):
- Even among people whose experience was mostly negative, 94 of 110 chose a limit, not removal.
- CCF and feeders say bites come from a few dogs, and moving a pack only makes it fight.
- Feeding zones exist, but sit next to garbage or behind a small board, so dogs get fed near rooms instead.

Under them, "Where our sources disagree", four rows drawn as small two-way signposts, terracotta arrow pointing left, teal arrow pointing right:
- Sterilisation: CCF: almost all females are sterilised ← → Guards: litters keep coming
- Boundary: Gate guard: outside dogs are kept out ← → CCF: a gate is broken, the wall has gaps
- Shelter: CCF: a shelter takes medical cases ← → Caretaker: nowhere dogs can stay
- Bites: CCF: rare ← → 13 of the first 36 recorded interviews mention one

### Right card: What we did, and our KPIs

Top, heading "What we did", three items each with a tiny flat icon (poster on a wall, a speech bubble, an envelope):
- Put up and handed out posters across campus showing the designated feeding spots.
- Told students and residents what CCF is and what it does.
- Sent CCF a written report with our proposed solutions.

Then heading "What we asked CCF for", on a pinned-note style card:
- Regular communication between CCF and the administration, so students hear one message.
- More CCF initiatives, and more awareness of CCF among students and residents.
- Feeding-zone information made prominent: clear signs, a map, fixed times.
- Stricter rules on feeding in unauthorised places, and on petting or keeping dogs inside hostels.
