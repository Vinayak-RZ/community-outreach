Design an A3 portrait poster (297 × 420 mm, 300 dpi), "Campus dogs: two sides of one street", for EEC 399 Community Outreach, Group 23, IIT Roorkee. This is a design-led poster that stops people walking past. Build it around one visual idea, below, and keep every block readable from 1.5 m. Use only the text below. Do not add facts, numbers, quotes, names or sections.

## The idea: the poster is a street seen from above

- A charcoal asphalt road (#2B2B2E, about 70 mm wide) runs down the centre of the sheet from the top edge to two-thirds of the way down, with a mustard (#E8B03A) dashed centre line.
- The left pavement is tinted warm terracotta (#F3DED2) and belongs to the Against side. The right pavement is tinted soft teal (#D6E9E4) and belongs to the For side.
- Just under the title, one white single-line drawing of a dog, mid-stride, is crossing the centre line. That is the only dog drawing on the sheet.
- Two-thirds down, the road meets a zebra crossing (white stripes across the full width). Below the crossing, the sides merge: the map, the work we did and the KPIs sit on a shared off-white ground (#FAF6EF). That is where both sides meet.

Type: a tall condensed display face (Anton or Bebas Neue) for the title and the big numbers; a handwritten face (Caveat) only for the For quotes; a heavy stencil or slab (Black Ops One or Roboto Slab Black) only for the Against quotes; Inter or Open Sans for everything else, body 13–15 pt, source labels 10 pt grey.

Do not use: emoji, paw prints, cartoon dogs with faces, stock dog photos, gradients, team photos. British spelling.

### Numbers painted on the road (about 90 mm)

Down the road, four stats painted like road markings: a huge white number in the display face, the label in small white caps under it, the fraction in mustard.

Beside them on each pavement, three short insight lines in body type.

Left pavement, small terracotta heading "Why this side is angry":
- The hardest lines come from people who were chased, mostly at night, on a few roads.
- Dogs inside hostels, in washrooms and on beds, is the most common complaint.
- Mess, canteen and cleaning staff were the most negative group we spoke to.

Right pavement, small teal heading "Why this side holds firm":
- Even among people whose experience was mostly negative, 94 of 110 chose a limit, not removal.
- CCF and feeders say bites come from a few dogs, and moving a pack only makes it fight.
- Feeding zones exist, but sit next to garbage or behind a small board, so dogs get fed near rooms instead.

Tiny grey source line at the foot of the road: Google Form, 211 responses (209 students). Interviews with students, guards, staff, CCF, feeders and the CMO.
