# Sheet 02 — Two sides

_Not the sheet to build. The maintained form for the one A3 is [`improve-poster-prompt.md`](improve-poster-prompt.md). That sheet includes the KPI table and the previous campus map._

A3 portrait, 297 × 420 mm. Hung in the middle. If the committee takes one sheet, it is this one. Slide 8 of `course/course-deck.txt` names the topic "Two sides of one street" and asks for the feeders' view and the afraid side. This sheet is that ask.

The course wants the psychology of people we actually spoke to. The page is built from interviews and from two survey lines kept in the writer's spelling. Interview lines are paraphrases. `analysis/interview-coding.md` says none of them has been checked against the audio. Quotation marks are reserved for the survey rows.

## What the sheet says

**Walk-by:**

One side stays after a bite.
The other changes the road.

"Changes the road" is the only oxide on the sheet, `oklch(0.47 0.11 38)`, same size as the rest of the sentence. Barlow Condensed, 48 pt, weight 700. Two lines. Nothing else in the top 72 mm except the sheet number "02" at 11 pt, aligned with 01 and 03.

The bottom 32 mm is one sentence across both columns, 16 pt:

Out of rooms. Fed away from doors. Boundary shut.

That sentence is the plan both sides could sign. It is the hinge into sheet 03. It is not repeated as a heading over there.

## How the page is built

The split is unequal, because the two sides are not the same kind of evidence.

| Side | Width | Who | Why this width |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | 96 mm | People who look after dogs | Fewer voices, longer reasons. A narrow column forces short lines. |
| Right | 156 mm | People who fear the street or live with the mess | Three different experiences: the chase, the room, the bin. They need room. |

Gutter 15 mm. No cards, no icons, no matching headings. The left heading is "Who feeds, and why they will not sign a clearance." The right heading is "What happens to the route." Different jobs, different grammar. Headings are Barlow 15 pt, weight 600. Body is Barlow 14 pt on the right and 13 pt on the left, leading 1.4. A column of 14 pt on 156 mm stays near 55 characters. Do not justify.

Hairline between the columns: 0.25 mm, colour of the paper's grey `oklch(0.86 0.012 85)`. Not a coloured bar.

### Left column

Five short blocks, 10 mm apart. Sources in 9 pt mute at the end of the column, not after every line.

- They feed, medicate, and put water out because water is not provided. CCF says a fixed spot, actually used, makes dogs less ready to chase. Source: `analysis/interview-coding.md` I07, I23, I24.
- They locate the harm in two places: gaps in the boundary, and food left at rooms and doors. Source: `analysis/synthesis.md` F4, F5.
- They will not sign a clearance. Dogs are territorial. A moved pack fights. They expect rats and monkeys if the dogs go. Source: `analysis/interview-coding.md` I24; `project-brief.md` (CCF Meet, paraphrased).
- A senior who was bitten three times still asks for designated spots, sterilisation, dogs out of canteens, and a first-year guideline. The recording is reliability C. This is the gist, not a quote. Source: `analysis/interview-coding.md` I35.
- Stance of the people we coded, so the column is not only CCF: feeders and caretakers lean toward dogs staying with management (2 interviews). CCF is the strongest pro-dog group we spoke to (3). Students as a group sit near the middle. Source: `analysis/interview-coding.md`, stance means.

One survey line, kept as typed, 13 pt, set with extra space above it so it is not a pull-quote card:

"throwing them out of the campus will be really inhumane" (row 179)

### Right column

The first line is the psychology, before any list:

A chase rewrites the night. People who were chased rate this as serious. People who were not, mostly do not.

Then the figure, in the same size as the body, not as a second hero number: 112 of 129 who were chased rated the problem 4 or 5. 18 of 82 who were not chased did. Source: `analysis/synthesis.md` F3.

Then three blocks that do not share a template:

- **The route.** Students name packs at night on a few roads: MAC and the road toward Rajiv, MNC toward Govind, SAC, the Mandir, the library route. Some stop walking alone from the lab. Two interviews describe a fall while running from a dog, not a bite. Source: `analysis/synthesis.md` F3; `analysis/interview-coding.md` I03, I08, I12, I28, I30, I33.
- **The room.** 9 of 18 students we interviewed brought up dogs inside hostels. Dogs in rooms, on beds, in washrooms and labs. Survey line, as typed: "i can't use the washroom in peace in my own bhawan because these dogs eat the used sanitary pads." (row 68). A second line, as typed, because it is a fix someone already saw: "Once the dustbin were place at a height, dogs stopped entering our Bhawan." (row 112). Source: `analysis/interview-coding.md`; `analysis/survey-analysis.md` §6.
- **The people in the middle.** Guards (5 interviews): 3 mention bites, 3 mention chases. They can shoo a dog and report upward. Field notes, unattributed, so labelled as notes: students press them not to act, and the duty to keep the path safe stays theirs. Mess, shop and cleaning staff (4) are the most negative group we spoke to. Their subject is dirt, bins and food. 3 of 4 want fewer dogs. Source: `analysis/interview-coding.md` I08, I11, I13, I17, I18, I20, I21, I30; `project-brief.md` (Comm Kaam guard notes).

Students, for scale, in one mute line at the foot of the right column: of 18 student interviews, 8 want dogs out of hostels, 7 want designated zones, 2 ask for fewer dogs or removal. Even among survey respondents whose experience was mostly negative, 94 of 110 still chose a coexistence option. Source: `analysis/interview-coding.md`; `analysis/synthesis.md` F1.

A bitten student waited until morning because a physician had to see them before the vaccine, though emergency is open through the night. Do not print a dose count. The spot-check says the schedule needs a listen. Source: `analysis/synthesis.md` F7; `transcripts/SPOT-CHECKS.md` (Malishka pt1).
