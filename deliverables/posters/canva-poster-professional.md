Design an A3 landscape academic poster (420 × 297 mm, 300 dpi) titled "Campus dogs: two sides of one street" for EEC 399 Community Outreach, Group 23, IIT Roorkee. It should read like a polished conference research poster: a solid header band, content in titled panels on a clear grid, plenty of white space, every number easy to find, nothing decorative that carries no information.

Copy rules:
- The six quotes on the two sides are final. Print them exactly as written here, word for word, with their spelling, capitals and punctuation. Do not correct, shorten or restyle their wording.
- Everything else in this prompt is draft copy. You may shorten it, reword it or split it into shorter lines so it fits the layout and reads well on a poster. Keep every number, fraction, date, name and fact as it is, and keep the meaning of each line.
- Do not add facts, numbers, quotes, names or sections that are not in this prompt.
- Do not add icons beyond the ones described.

## Look

- Background warm off-white #FAF6EF. Panels are white cards, 3 mm radius, thin #E3DDD2 border, no shadows.
- Structure colour deep navy #1F2A44 for the header band and panel title bars (white text).
- Side colours, only on the Against and For panels: terracotta #C2502E (Against), deep teal #1F6F6B (For).
- Highlight mustard #E8B03A, only for the big numbers.
- Type: Montserrat (or similar geometric sans) for headings and numbers; Inter or Open Sans for body. Title 72 pt bold, panel titles 22 pt, quotes 20–24 pt, body 12–14 pt, source labels 9 pt grey #6B6B6B.
- Flat, minimal line icons are allowed only as small markers in the KPI table rows. No emoji, paw prints, illustrations, stock photos, gradients or team photos. British spelling.
- Margins 12 mm, gutters 6 mm.

## Grid (landscape, top to bottom)

1. Header band, full width, about 34 mm.
2. Hook row, about 88 mm: Against panel (left, 40%) · four number tiles stacked in a narrow centre column (20%) · For panel (right, 40%).
3. Evidence row, about 112 mm: map (left, 42%) · disagreements above "What we did and proposed" (middle, 26%) · KPI table (right, 32%).
4. Timeline strip, full width, about 22 mm.
5. Footer line, about 9 mm.

### 3. Evidence row

**Map panel**, title bar: Where the dogs gather

Place the uploaded map image as is. Do not redraw, recolour or crop the legend.

Caption, 11 pt: Pins rank where we saw the most dogs (1 highest, 3 lowest). Green triangles are feeding spots as told to us by CCF and feeders. The gap between them is the problem: dogs gather near hostels and activity centres, not only where feeding is allowed. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors.

**Middle column, top panel**, title bar: Where our sources disagree

Four rows, bold topic, then two short opposing lines, a terracotta dot before one and a teal dot before the other:
- **Sterilisation.** CCF: almost all female dogs are sterilised. Guards: litters keep coming.
- **Boundary.** A gate guard: outside dogs are kept out. CCF: a gate is broken and the wall has gaps.
- **Shelter.** CCF: a shelter takes medical cases. A caretaker: there is nowhere dogs can stay.
- **Bites.** CCF: rare. 13 of the first 36 recorded interviews mention one. Hospital figures were not shared.

**Middle column, bottom panel**, title bar: What we did and proposed

What we did:
- Put up and handed out posters across campus showing the designated feeding spots.
- Told students and residents what CCF is and what it does.
- Sent CCF a written report with our proposed solutions.

What we proposed to CCF:
- Regular communication between CCF and the administration, so students hear one message.
- More CCF initiatives, and more awareness of CCF among students and residents.
- Feeding-zone information made prominent: clear signs, a map, fixed times.
- Stricter rules on feeding in unauthorised places, and on petting or keeping dogs inside hostels.
