## Look

- Background: warm off-white #FAF6EF. Panels: white cards, 4 mm radius, thin #E3DDD2 border, no shadows.
- Structure colour: deep navy #1F2A44 for the header band and the section title bars (white text).
- Two side colours, used only for the "Against" and "For" blocks: terracotta #C2502E (Against) and deep teal #1F6F6B (For).
- Highlight: mustard #E8B03A, only for the big numbers in Insights.
- Type: one geometric sans (Montserrat or similar) for headings and numbers, one clean sans (Open Sans or Inter) for body. Title 80 pt bold, section titles 28 pt, quotes 26–32 pt, body 13–15 pt, source labels 10 pt grey #6B6B6B.
- No emoji, no paw prints, no cartoon or stock dog photos, no gradients, no team photos. British spelling.
- Margins 15 mm, 8 mm gutters.

### 3. What we found

Section title bar: What we found

Four number tiles, each with a mustard number, a one-line label and the fraction in grey:

- **86%** want the dogs to stay, with limits. 181 of 211. Only 17 chose free movement.
- **68%** have had a dog come into their hostel or building. 143 of 211.
- **87% vs 22%** call dogs a serious problem: those who were chased (112 of 129) against those who were not (18 of 82).
- **71%** had not heard of CCF or of sterilisation drives. 149 of 211.

Strip below, two columns of short insights:

Left column, heading "Why the Against side is angry":
- The hardest lines come from people who were chased, mostly at night, on a few roads.
- Dogs inside hostels, in washrooms and on beds, is the most common complaint.
- Mess, canteen and cleaning staff were the most negative group we spoke to.

Right column, heading "Why the For side holds firm":
- Even among people whose experience was mostly negative, 94 of 110 chose a limit, not removal.
- CCF and feeders say bites come from a few dogs, and that moving a pack only makes it fight.
- Feeding zones exist, but people told us they sit next to garbage or behind a small board, so dogs are fed near rooms instead.

Source line under the strip, grey: Google Form, 211 responses (209 students). Interviews with students, guards, staff, CCF, feeders and the CMO.

Right panel, title bar: Where our sources disagree

Four rows, each a bold topic, then two short opposing lines (terracotta dot for one side, teal dot for the other):

**Sterilisation.** CCF: almost all female dogs are sterilised. Guards: litters keep coming.
**Boundary.** A gate guard: outside dogs are kept out. CCF: a gate is broken and the wall has gaps.
**Shelter.** CCF: a shelter takes medical cases. A caretaker: there is nowhere dogs can stay.
**Bites.** CCF: rare. 13 of 36 early interviews mention one. The hospital figures were not shared.

### 5. What we did, and the KPI table

Left panel, title bar: What we did and proposed

What we did:
- Put up posters across campus showing the designated feeding spots, and handed them out to residents.
- Told students and residents what CCF is and what it does.
- Sent CCF a written report with our proposed solutions.

What we proposed to CCF:
- Regular communication between CCF and the administration, so students hear one message.
- More CCF initiatives, and more awareness of CCF among students and residents.
- Feeding-zone information made prominent: clear signs, a map, fixed times.
- Stricter rules on feeding in unauthorised places and on petting or keeping dogs inside hostels.
