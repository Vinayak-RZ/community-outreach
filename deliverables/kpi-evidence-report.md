Department of Electrical Engineering, IIT Roorkee · Autumn 2026
Vansh Gautam, Vinayak Raizada, Jay D, Vrinda Gupta, Jiya Dubey, Khushi, Nandini

This report walks through the five indicators we used for the outreach work, with the evidence behind each one. It sits with the project Drive and the A3 poster as part of the course record.

## Reach

Fifty people were spoken to on campus. Separately, a Google Form ran from 13 to 17 October 2026 and brought in 211 responses.

Wherever consent and circumstances allowed, conversations went on tape. Forty-three were recorded and coded; seven were written up when recording was not possible. One recording covers two students, so the recorded set stands for at least 44 people. Of the 211 form responses, 209 came from students, one from a faculty member and one from a staff member.

Students make up 24 of the recorded conversations — eighteen in October, six in November. Guards: nine in total (six on tape, three written up), mostly at Govind Bhawan, Mechanical, and the Himalaya / Aravali stretch, each talk roughly forty minutes. Mess, canteen and sanitation staff: five, four of them recorded, including one at Jawahar canteen. The rest of the recorded set includes three people from CCF, two feeders or caretakers and one faculty member; three talks could not be placed in a group.

Nine people we spoke to had themselves been bitten, scratched or attacked — seven on tape, two in notes. The Chief Medical Officer appears in notes from 13 October, then in a longer conversation on a third visit on 24–25 October, when recording was not allowed.

Some people sit in more than one of these groups. Interview codes are kept throughout so any figure here can be traced to a file.

## Output

Two things went back out: feeding-zone posters put up across campus, and a solutions report sent to CCF.

| No. | Point | No. | Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Govind · DS Barracks | 5 | Gate 5 · iHUB, near Kasturba |
| 2 | Himalaya · KIH | 6 | PNB · Green Gate |
| 3 | Sheel Kunj Gate | 7 | MAC Theatre |
| 4 | Sarojini Water Tank | 8 | Cautley Mess |

Each poster shows the eight points, a map, and four rules: feed only at the marked zones; this spot is one of them; leave the place clean; if a dog approaches, stay calm, look away and back away slowly.

They went out two ways. Copies were taped to notice boards, pillars, a water cooler and a gate. Handouts were given to people during the night drive so they could read the rule and the map on the spot.

The solutions report asked CCF for four things: steadier communication with the administration, and more initiatives from CCF’s side; wider awareness of CCF among students and residents; feeding-zone information made obvious through signs, a map and fixed times; and stricter rules against feeding in unauthorised places and against petting or keeping dogs inside hostels.

## Change

Before the campaign, 149 of the 211 people who answered the survey had not heard of CCF or of organised sterilisation.

That is why the poster lists the eight points. The gap was not disagreement about dogs so much as missing information — most respondents did not know who manages the campus dog population, or where feeding is allowed.

On the night drive, people stopped at the posters and read the handouts. Dated photographs show residents reading the handout on the road, and posters up at eight places across campus.

From what we saw that night, people who did not know the zones picked them up from the poster; more people recognised CCF; and feeding away from the marked spots looked less common once the rule and the map were where people walk. We also asked residents to think about what the campus community can do about the condition of its dogs.

We are relying on what we saw that night and on those photographs. No written reply has come from CCF or from an office, and none is claimed here.
