# Next steps

What we plan to do next, in rough order. These follow on from the 36 interviews (18 students, 1 faculty) and the survey. None of this has been done yet.

## 1. Ear-notch and hotspot walk
Walk the hotspot routes in the evening and again at night: outside Himalaya Bhawan, outside MAC, around the Architecture dept, between Govind Bhawan and Chemistry, and near Jawahar Bhawan. Count dogs, note how many have an ear notch, and take geotagged photos. This gives us real counts to put next to what people told us.

## 2. Findings note to CCF or the Chief Warden (Output evidence)
Send a short findings note (one page) and get a written acknowledgement back. A reply email is enough.

## 3. One small commitment in writing (Change evidence)
Ask a warden or CCF for one small, specific commitment in writing, for example fixing one feeding spot or checking one boundary gap.

## 4. Written data requests
- Hospital: bite cases and anti-rabies doses, by month if possible.
- Security: dog complaints logged.

## 5. 6–8 more interviews to fill gaps
- a warden
- the CMO or a doctor, on record
- the Security officer
- someone from estate or works about gate 7
- women students from the girls' bhawans
- someone from the MNC area

## 6. Interviews at the hotspots
Talk to people at Himalaya, MAC, Architecture, Govind–Chemistry and Jawahar, and note the time of day for each.

## 7. Follow-up poll
A short poll with a neutral option and a removal option, plus hostel and gender columns. Keep it open over several days so one day's mood doesn't skew it.

## 8. Small before/after pilot
Pick one:
- feeding-zone signs at one spot, or
- an "if bitten" card at one bhawan.

Note the situation before, put it up, and check again after.

## 9. Awareness session or stall with CCF
Run it with CCF and keep an attendance sign-in sheet.
