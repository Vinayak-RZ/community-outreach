## Codebook
| Code | Theme | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| P-BITE | People's problems | Bite or scratch mentioned: to the speaker, someone they know, or one they saw or heard of |
| P-CHASE | People's problems | Chased or charged, packs at night, vehicles chased |
| P-FEAR | People's problems | Fear, avoiding routes or times, lasting wariness |
| P-NOISE | People's problems | Barking or fights at night |
| P-HYG | People's problems | Fouling, dustbins tipped, dogs on beds or in washrooms, mess and food hygiene |
| P-HOSTEL | People's problems | Dogs inside hostels, rooms, labs or buildings |
| P-MED | People's problems | Post-bite care: vaccine course, cost, waiting for a doctor |
| P-INJ-OTHER | People's problems | Injury that isn't a bite (falling while fleeing a dog) |
| D-FOOD | Dogs' problems | Hunger, water, feeding |
| D-INJ | Dogs' problems | Injury or illness, medical care |
| D-STER | Dogs' problems | Sterilisation or vaccination status |
| D-SHEL | Dogs' problems | No shelter, or NGOs won't take them |
| D-MIS | Dogs' problems | Mistreatment, being driven out or hit, killing ruled out |
| C-FEED | Causes | Feeding outside the zones, near rooms, or zones too few or poorly marked |
| C-WASTE | Causes | Open bins, food and non-veg waste, mess leftovers |
| C-BOUND | Causes | Boundary gaps, broken gate or wall, outside dogs coming in |
| C-BREED | Causes | Breeding, dumped puppies, numbers rising |
| C-FEW | Causes | A few aggressive dogs or particular packs cause most trouble |
| S-OUT | Solutions | Keep dogs out of hostels, messes, canteens |
| S-ZONE | Solutions | Designated areas, or enforced feeding zones and times |
| S-STER | Solutions | Sterilise or vaccinate (incl. collar tags or ear-notch) |
| S-SHEL | Solutions | Shelter or separate arrangement |
| S-AWARE | Solutions | Awareness, how to behave around dogs, first-year guidelines |
| S-REDUCE | Solutions | Reduce numbers, or remove dogs from campus |
| S-REMOVE-AGG | Solutions | Remove or relocate only the aggressive dogs |
| S-BOUND | Solutions | Seal the boundary and gates |
| S-ADMIN | Solutions | A permanent arrangement from the administration, or criticism that it isn't acting |

**Stance** (coder's judgement of the speaker's overall position): −2 remove dogs from campus · −1 restrict hard or reduce numbers · 0 coexist with limits, or mixed · +1 pro-dog with management · +2 dogs' rights first.
**Reliability:** A = readable English or a clean re-run · B = gist readable, some garbling · C = heavy garbling, gaps or loops, gist only.

## What the coding shows (detail in `synthesis.md`)
- **Students** mostly raise dogs in hostels (9/18) and fear (9/18), and ask for "out of hostels" (8/18) plus designated zones (7/18). Almost no student asks to remove dogs altogether (2/18 reduce/remove).
- **Guards** report the most bites and chasing (3/5 each) and point to outside dogs and boundary gaps (2/5). They can only shoo dogs and report upward.
- **Mess, shop and cleaning staff** are the most negative group (mean −1.0; 3/4 want fewer dogs). Their complaints are hygiene and food.
- **CCF** puts causes first (feeding outside zones and boundary gaps, 2/3 each), and is the main source on sterilisation, vaccination and shelter.
- **Shared ground across every group:** feeding zones that actually work, dogs kept out of living and food spaces, and a sealed boundary.

## Limitations
One coder, coding from machine transcripts. 14 of 36 interviews are reliability C. Group sizes are small: treat counts as a description of *who said what to us*, not as rates. Interviewer prompts often suggested answers (e.g. VID-20261016-WA0018, where interviewers argue for a reduction), which pushes the stance scores. **Next step:** a second team member re-codes 10 interviews and checks the C-rated ones by ear.
