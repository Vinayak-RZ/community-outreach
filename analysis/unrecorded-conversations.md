## Hospital doctor

Remembered, not taped.

- Dog bites are handled in three levels. The older note's phrase "three class dogs" (`project-brief.md`, CMO, 13/10) may be the same point mis-heard. Neither version is a protocol we can quote.
- Cases are handled on campus. The recollection is that people rarely had to be sent to a hospital outside.
- A single bite is expensive to treat. The figure was remembered as "three twenty thousand". That string is not settled: it may be ₹3,20,000, or a range in the thousands. Do not print either reading as the hospital's number.
- Bite cases were remembered as "upwards of two hundred or three hundred a year", and as rising in recent years. This is memory of what was said. It is not the hospital register. It sits beside, and does not replace, the second-hand claims already marked unverified in `synthesis.md` F7.
- A dog bite was compared with a snake bite, and called more dangerous. The reason given: a person is afraid of a snake at once and takes care, and is not afraid of a dog in the same way. The bite may not show itself quickly, and if it is not treated early the risk named was rabies, and death. This is the recollection behind the hook the team has been using. It is still not a quote, and the doctor is not named.

## Govind Bhawan guard

Remembered from a talk of about 40 minutes. He was described as having worked on campus for over fifteen years.

- About seven or eight years ago, dogs were removed from the campus. A campus community opposed that removal. The dogs came back. This is his account, not a dated office record.
- He is against dogs on campus because he ranks human life above the dogs'.
- A specific story: a professor's daughter was surrounded by dogs. He went to move the dogs off her. She shouted at him for hurting and scaring them. A few days later the dogs attacked her. He told this as a case of someone who cares for dogs still being attacked.
- Do not put this story on a poster next to his bhawan. `course/course-deck.txt` slide 20: a quote that identifies a person to their employer stays off. In a viva it can be told as an unrecorded account from a long-serving hostel guard, with the bhawan omitted if the committee might connect it to one man.

## Jawahar canteen

Canteen workers have trouble with dogs. On the way to the canteen there are enough dogs that some people will not walk there alone. This is the recollection. It is not the Sarojini or VK canteen transcripts.

## The injured dog, early in the project

A woman was standing among dogs. The team first read her as either afraid or there to feed. She was searching for one brown dog, injured, that had run from a group of five or six. She had come to help it. A team member helped spot it. Getting the dog settled took over half an hour. She is described as a PhD student of this institute, and the talk with her as recorded. Until that file is matched in `transcripts/`, the rescue is a team account and the interview is not yet evidence.

## What the guards would not say into a microphone

From the same account, as an observation rather than a quote: guards and canteen workers, spoken to casually, treat dogs as a menace. Asked to say that on a recording, they change the statement. The reason given is pressure from the administration. Asked about institute guidelines, the guards' account is that the rules are not applied strictly even when a dog is harming a student or creating a hygiene problem in a hostel.

This observation explains a split already in the files, without proving it. Coded guard interviews are mixed: some want fewer dogs, one argues for feeding (`interview-coding.md` I08, I11, I13). The Comm Kaam field note already says guards want complete removal and that dog lovers stop them acting (`project-brief.md`, Comm Kaam). The new point is the mechanism: the milder on-record line may be the safe line, not the whole opinion. It is one team's reading of conversations they could not tape. Say that when you use it.

## What this does, and does not, change

It does not create a bite total, a cost per bite, or a dog count. Those remain memory until the hospital or the guard's approved notes are in the repo.

It does add contact the KPI table does not yet count. The claimed time is on the order of an hour at the hospital, about 40 minutes with each of four guards and two victims, plus a canteen talk and a half-hour rescue. That is not person-hours. There is no diary row, no start and end time, and no second person confirming the clock. `deliverables/kpi-table.md` stays as written.

It does mean the hook is no longer only a rumour inside the team. A member who was in the hospital room, and a member who heard the Govind guard, have now stated both stories, and have stated why there is no tape. The honest form is: "We were told this. They would not be recorded. We have no document."

## How to say it

Usable, with the label on it:

- The hospital handles bite cases on campus, in three levels, and described a dog bite as more dangerous than a snake bite because people do not fear the dog.
- A long-serving guard says an earlier removal did not last, and tells of being shouted at for moving dogs off a student who was later attacked.
- Guards and canteen staff sound harsher off the record than on it, and describe the institute's own rules as weakly applied.
- Two further bite or scratch accounts, at Jawahar and Ganga, were heard and not taped.
- Getting to the canteen alone is, for some people, already the problem.

Not usable as a fact:

- Any rupee figure, and any "200 or 300 a year".
- The name of the doctor, the guard, the professor, or the professor's child.
- A claim that the approved guard notes have been read. They were described, and they are not in the repo.
