# Synthesis: what the fieldwork says (group 23, campus dogs)

_Drafted 20/10/2026 by Acads for the team to check. Sources: survey n = 211 ([`survey-analysis.md`](survey-analysis.md)), 36 coded interviews ([`interview-coding.md`](interview-coding.md), IDs I01–I36), the CMO notes of 13/10 ([`../project-brief.md`](../project-brief.md)), the CCF Meet of 24/9 ([`../source/ccf-gmeet-transcript.txt`](../source/ccf-gmeet-transcript.txt), the same conversation as I24) and Vinayak's field notes ([`../source/gandagi.md`](../source/gandagi.md)). Every interview point is a paraphrase from a machine transcript. Check the audio before quoting anything._

**Labels:** **[S]** survey · **[I]** interviews · **[CCF]** CCF office-bearer or volunteer · **[G]** guards · **[CMO]** CMO notes · **unverified** means a claim with no record behind it yet.

## Key findings

1. **The real conflict is about space, not whether dogs belong.**
   - 85.8% of survey respondents chose a managed-coexistence boundary and only 8.1% chose free movement [S §4.2].
   - Students in interviews mostly say "fine on the roads, not in the hostels": 8 of 18 student interviews ask for S-OUT [I].
   - Removal is a real minority view, though. 26 of 98 free-text answers propose it [S §6.1], and the form had no removal option, so the closed question undercounts it.

2. **Dogs inside hostels and rooms are the most common problem people actually live with.**
   - 67.8% of the survey have had dogs enter their hostel or building [S].
   - 11 of 36 interviews describe dogs in rooms, labs or washrooms, fouling floors and beds, or carrying off shoes [I: I01, I05, I08, I09, I32, I33, I34].
   - Guards say all they can do is shoo the dogs and report upward [G: I08 0:37].

3. **Fear is driven by being chased, mostly at night and on a few routes.**
   - Respondents who felt unsafe or were chased rate dogs a 4–5 problem 86.8% of the time, against 22.0% of the rest [S §4.1].
   - Interviews name the same places as the survey free text: the MAC open area and the MAC–Rajiv Bhawan road, MNC to Govind, SAC, the Mandir, the library route and gates where bikes get chased [I: I03, I06, I13, I19, I23, I33; S §6].
   - Late-night lab users describe detours and waiting for packs to move off [I33].
   - Not every injury is a bite. People fall while running from dogs [I08, I28, I30].

4. **Most of the trouble is blamed on a few packs and on dogs from outside, not on "the dogs".**
   - The CCF office-bearer and volunteer say bites come from a few aggressive dogs and from unneutered dogs from outside [CCF: I23 3:49–4:09; I24].
   - Guards blame some 8–10 outside dogs [G: I11 1:03].
   - The caretaker points to boundary gaps, a broken wall and gate 7, and puppies dumped at night [I25 1:43, 5:24, 8:06].
   - The CCF office-bearer says gate 7 is broken [I24 12:31–12:38].
   - This view comes from several directions, but no one has counted it.

5. **Feeding is both the fix and the cause.**
   - Everyone agrees dogs should be fed at zones. Nine interviews say feeding outside the zones, or near rooms, draws dogs to people [I: C-FEED].
   - Students and a guard say the zones are badly made or marked: next to garbage piles [I04], "a tiny board" [I10, I35], "not properly made" [I13 3:15–3:19].
   - The survey says the same: "feeding zones need to be seriously enforced" (survey row 118, written text) [S §6].
   - Open bins and food waste come up in 6 interviews. One respondent reports that raising the dustbins stopped dogs entering their bhawan (row 112) [S].

6. **Awareness is low on both sides.**
   - 70.6% of the survey didn't know about sterilisation or CCF [S §4.6].
   - Several students hadn't heard of CCF, or had heard of it but never seen it act [I04, I05, I22].
   - The CCF side, for its part, asks for empathy and reads barking as territorial rather than aggressive [I23]. People who fear dogs say they aren't being heard [I04].

7. **Facts on record are missing, and post-bite care is a weak spot.**
   - We have no hospital or admin numbers. Every number we have is second-hand and **unverified**:
     - "bite cases rising over 1–2 years" [G: I11 0:47]
     - "five rabies cases since last December" and a vaccine shortage [CCF: I23 1:47–1:57]
     - "~90 female dogs" [CCF: I24 14:50]
   - Two bitten students describe the vaccine course. One waited until the next morning to see a physician [I12 pt1 1:39–1:50; I03].
   - The CMO notes say the first dose should be given within 2 days [CMO].
   - A guard says a bite means many expensive injections [I13 2:06].

## Survey limitations (keep these on the poster as a footnote)
- **Leading items.** There was no removal option. "Strongest reason dogs have a place" assumes they have one. The response sheet is titled "Removal of dogs from campus" [S §7].
- **Timing and sample.** 199 of 211 responses (94.3%) arrived on 13/10, the day the form went round the WhatsApp groups. 99.1% are students, self-selected. Call it "211 respondents", not "campus opinion".
- **Missing fields.** There's no bhawan, gender, year, or bite question, so no hostel or gender breakdown is possible.
- **Coding.** The free text was coded by one person, and the keyword counts are crude.
- **Interviews.** These are a convenience sample, mostly on the 14/10 night. 14 of 36 are hard to read (reliability C). Some interviewer prompts were leading.

## Recommendations (humane, practical, tied to evidence)
| # | Recommendation | Evidence | Who acts |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Close hostel entrances to dogs, not campus to dogs.** Fit mesh or self-closing doors at bhawan entrances and messes, and raise or cover the bins. One bhawan already has a net on its fence [I12 pt2]. Raised bins worked (survey row 112). CCF mentions meshes at another IIT [I23] | S 67.8% dogs in hostels; S-OUT 10/36; P-HOSTEL 11/36 | Chief Warden, bhawan wardens, Estate |
| R2 | **Feeding zones that work.** Fewer, signed, larger zones away from paths, hostel doors and garbage, with fixed times. Feeders agree to feed only there | C-FEED 9/36; S-ZONE 11/36; zones next to garbage and boards too small [I04, I10, I35] | CCF with Estate. Registered feeders |
| R3 | **Repair the boundary.** Fix gate 7, the Himalaya-side railing and the broken wall, so outside dogs and dumped puppies can't come in | I23, I24, I25, I11 (outside dogs); C-BOUND 5/36 | Estate office, Security |
| R4 | **Open the sterilisation and vaccination record.** Publish the counts once a year. Agree who runs the drives (CCF or Security). Use an ear-notch as the visible marker | Sterilisation conflict above; 70.6% unaware [S] | CCF, Security, with the Dean of Students |
| R5 | **A clear post-bite route, 24×7.** Put up a one-page notice in every bhawan: where to go at night, the first-dose window, the full course | I12, I03, I34 (P-MED 6/36); CMO notes | Institute hospital (CMO) |
| R6 | **A report line for a specific aggressive dog or pack.** It leads to humane handling (CCF or vet assessment, then relocation under the applicable animal-birth-control rules; the team should check the current rules before citing them), not to culling. People are told what happened | C-FEW 6/36; S-REMOVE-AGG 4/36; guards can only shoo [I08] | Security, CCF |
| R7 | **Two-way awareness at orientation.** For first-years: how to behave around dogs, the hotspot routes at night, who CCF is. For feeders: why feeding at doors hurts the dogs' case | S 70.6% unaware; S-AWARE 7/36; I35 (guidelines for first-years) | CCF, student council, Dean of Students |

**Does technology help?** (the course asks this.) Mostly no. The fixes are physical (doors, bins, walls) and organisational (zones, records). Two small tools could help: a QR form for reporting an aggressive dog or a gap in the boundary, and a shared hotspot map. Only 2 of 98 survey answers suggested technology [S §6.2]. Saying this honestly is itself a result.
