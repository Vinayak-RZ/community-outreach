## How to read the findings
**Strength** counts how many of the 7 source types agree: survey, student interviews, guards, mess/shop/cleaning staff, CCF (volunteer, office-bearer, GMeet), feeders and faculty, and the CMO notes.
- **Strong**: 5 or more source types, including the survey.
- **Moderate**: 3–4 source types.
- **Weak**: 1–2 source types, or second-hand only.

Interview counts use the 36 coded interviews (denominators by group: students 18, guards 5, staff 4, CCF 3, feeders 2, faculty 1, unclear 3).

**Survey columns available:** role, experience, problems ticked, reason, boundary, CCF awareness, problem rating and free text. **There is no hostel, gender or year column**, so no breakdown by those is possible. The cross-tabs below use being chased or not, and experience.

## Findings

### F1. The conflict is over *space*, not whether dogs belong. **Strong (6/7)**
- **Survey.** 181/211 (85.8%) chose a managed-coexistence boundary: out of hostels 75, designated areas 54, vaccinated or sterilised only 52. 17/211 (8.1%) chose free movement.
- **Survey, by experience.** Even among the *mostly negative*, 94/110 (85.5%) chose coexistence options. Among the *mostly positive*, 33/46 (71.7%) still chose a restriction [survey §4.2].
- **Interviews.**
  - Students: "out of hostels" S-OUT 8/18, designated zones S-ZONE 7/18. Only 2/18 ask for fewer dogs or removal.
  - Guards: 1/5 for reduction (I11, 1–2 dogs per bhawan).
  - Faculty and feeders: let dogs stay with management (I02, I25).
- **Who disagrees.**
  - Survey free text: 26/98 (26.5%) propose removing all or most dogs. All 16 sole-removal answers come from *mostly negative* respondents. The form offered no removal option, so this is undercounted.
  - Staff: 3/4 want fewer dogs. One hostel staff member wants them removed by vehicle (I30).
  - CCF (I07, I24): dogs have a right to stay. They accept limits but frame them as feeding discipline.
- **So what →** Frame the proposal as *where dogs may be*, not *whether*. This is the one framing a majority of every group accepts (R1, R2).

### F2. Dogs inside hostels and rooms is the most common lived problem. **Moderate (4/7)**
- **Survey.** 143/211 (67.8%) have had dogs enter their hostel or building. It is the most-ticked problem.
  - Those who ticked it chose "restricted from hostels" 63/143 (44.1%), against 12/68 (17.6%) of the rest [§4.5].
  - Even 10/46 *mostly positive* respondents report it [§4.4].
- **Interviews.** P-HOSTEL in 11/36: students 9/18, guards 1/5.
  - Dogs in rooms, on beds, in washrooms and labs (I01, I05, I08, I09, I32, I33, I34).
  - Cleaning staff describe dirt in bathrooms (I21).
- **Who disagrees.** No source denies it. The CCF office-bearer says students feed dogs and keep them in rooms (I24); the cause is disputed, the problem isn't.
- **So what →** Physical barriers at bhawan entrances and messes, plus covered or raised bins, are the single most-supported change (R1).

### F3. Fear is driven by being chased, mostly at night and on a few routes. **Moderate (4/7)**
- **Survey.** 129/211 (61.1%) felt unsafe or were chased.
  - Chased → problem rating 4–5 for 112/129 (86.8%, mean 4.36). Not chased → 18/82 (22.0%, mean 2.22) [§4.1].
  - The chased group chose "designated areas" 45/129 (34.9%), against 9/82 (11.0%).
  - All 7 typed-in "remove" answers come from the chased group.
- **Interviews.**
  - P-CHASE in 10/36 (guards 3/5, students 5/18). P-FEAR in 12/36 (students 9/18).
  - Named places: the MAC open area and the MAC–Rajiv road, MNC–Govind, SAC, the Mandir and the library route (I03, I06, I13, I19, I23, I33). These match places in the survey free text [§6].
  - Injuries that aren't bites: falls while running from a dog (I08, I28, I30).
- **Who disagrees.** Pro-dog students say reading dogs' body language avoids trouble (I10, I35). CCF reads barking as territorial, not aggressive (I23).
- **So what →** Target the few routes and the night hours: lighting, a guard presence at known spots, and route guidance for late-night lab users (R6, R7).

### F4. Most trouble is blamed on a few packs and on dogs from outside. **Moderate (4/7, not yet counted)**
- **Interviews.**
  - C-FEW 6/36: students 3, CCF 2.
  - C-BOUND 5/36: guards 2/5, CCF 2/3, feeders 1/2.
  - Specific claims: some 8–10 outside dogs (MAC guard, I11 1:03). Unneutered outside dogs bite (CCF volunteer, I23 3:49–4:09). Gate 7 is broken (I24 12:31–12:38). Wall and railing gaps, and puppies dumped at night (I25 1:43, 5:24, 8:06).
- **Survey.** Not asked.
- **Who disagrees.** The Nehru Road gate guard says outside dogs aren't let in (I14).
- **So what →** Repair the boundary, and survey it with geotagged photos to turn this from opinion into evidence (R3).

### F5. Feeding zones exist but don't work. **Strong (6/7)**
- **Interviews.**
  - C-FEED in 9/36: students 3, guards 2, staff 1, CCF 2, faculty 1.
  - The zones are next to garbage (I04) or poorly marked, with "a tiny board" (I10, I35, paraphrased). Zones are "not properly made" and dogs are fed outside them (MNC guard, I13 2:57–3:19). Students feed near their rooms (I30).
- **Survey.** Feeding zones or regular feeding come up in 16/98 free-text answers. Hygiene or waste comes up in 16/98, and 114/211 (54.0%) ticked hygiene/waste. One respondent reports raised dustbins stopped dogs entering a bhawan (row 112).
- **Who disagrees.** The CCF office-bearer says the spots are already placed in quiet, segregated areas (I24 8:20–8:32). They exist; the dispute is whether they're used and enforced.
- **So what →** Fewer, signed, enforced zones at fixed times, agreed between CCF and feeders, away from doors and garbage (R2).

### F6. Few people know that CCF or sterilisation exists. **Moderate (3/7)**
- **Survey.** 149/211 (70.6%) aren't aware.
  - Awareness: mostly negative 29/110 (26.4%), mostly positive 20/46 (43.5%).
  - Mean problem rating: aware 3.32, not aware 3.62 [§4.6].
- **Interviews.** Students unaware of CCF (I05, I22), or aware but never seen it act (I04). CCF itself agrees sensitisation is needed (I07). S-AWARE 7/36.
- **Who disagrees.** None.
- **So what →** Two-way orientation (R7): it is cheap, and both sides ask for it.

### F7. There are no facts on record, and post-bite care is a weak spot. **Moderate (4/7); every number is unverified**
- **Second-hand claims only:**
  - "bite cases rising over 1–2 years" (I11 0:47, attributed to the hospital)
  - "five rabies cases since last December" and a vaccine shortage (I23 1:47–1:57, attributed to a professor)
  - "~90 female dogs, all neutered" (I24 14:50)
  - A survey claim of a death (row 193): **unverified, keep it off the poster**
- **Post-bite care.**
  - P-MED in 6/36 interviews.
  - A bitten student waited until morning for a physician (I12 pt1 1:39–1:50).
  - The CMO notes say the first dose should be given within 2 days.
  - A guard says a bite needs many expensive injections (I13 2:06).
  - **The survey had no bite question.**
- **Who disagrees.** The CCF office-bearer says bites are exceptional (I24).
- **So what →** Make a written data request to the hospital and Security (KPI action 6), and a 24×7 post-bite notice (R5). A refusal also goes on the poster, as an honest result.

## Survey limitations (keep these on the poster as a footnote)
- **Leading items.** There was no removal option. "Strongest reason dogs have a place" assumes they have one. The response sheet is titled "Removal of dogs from campus" [S §7].
- **Timing and sample.** 199 of 211 responses (94.3%) arrived on 13/10, the day the form went round the WhatsApp groups. 99.1% are students, self-selected. Call it "211 respondents", not "campus opinion".
- **Missing fields.** There's no bhawan, gender, year, or bite question, so no hostel or gender breakdown is possible.
- **Coding.** The free text was coded by one person, and the keyword counts are crude.
- **Interviews.** These are a convenience sample, mostly on the 14/10 night. 14 of 36 are hard to read (reliability C). Some interviewer prompts were leading.

|---|---|---|---|

| R2 | **Feeding zones that work.** Fewer, signed, larger zones away from paths, hostel doors and garbage, with fixed times. Feeders agree to feed only there | C-FEED 9/36; S-ZONE 11/36; zones next to garbage and boards too small [I04, I10, I35] | CCF with Estate. Registered feeders |
| R3 | **Repair the boundary.** Fix gate 7, the Himalaya-side railing and the broken wall, so outside dogs and dumped puppies can't come in | I23, I24, I25, I11 (outside dogs); C-BOUND 5/36 | Estate office, Security |
| R4 | **Open the sterilisation and vaccination record.** Publish the counts once a year. Agree who runs the drives (CCF or Security). Use an ear-notch as the visible marker | Sterilisation conflict above; 70.6% unaware [S] | CCF, Security, with the Dean of Students |
| R5 | **A clear post-bite route, 24×7.** Put up a one-page notice in every bhawan: where to go at night, the first-dose window, the full course | I12, I03, I34 (P-MED 6/36); CMO notes | Institute hospital (CMO) |
| R6 | **A report line for a specific aggressive dog or pack.** It leads to humane handling (CCF or vet assessment, then relocation under the applicable animal-birth-control rules; the team should check the current rules before citing them), not to culling. People are told what happened | C-FEW 6/36; S-REMOVE-AGG 4/36; guards can only shoo [I08] | Security, CCF |
| R7 | **Two-way awareness at orientation.** For first-years: how to behave around dogs, the hotspot routes at night, who CCF is. For feeders: why feeding at doors hurts the dogs' case | S 70.6% unaware; S-AWARE 7/36; I35 (guidelines for first-years) | CCF, student council, Dean of Students |

**Does technology help?** (the course asks this.) Mostly no. The fixes are physical (doors, bins, walls) and organisational (zones, records). Two small tools could help: a QR form for reporting an aggressive dog or a gap in the boundary, and a shared hotspot map. Only 2 of 98 survey answers suggested technology [S §6.2]. Saying this honestly is itself a result.
