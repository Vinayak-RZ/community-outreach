# EEC 399 Community Outreach: Course Overview

Source: `course-deck.pptx` (22 slides, extracted verbatim to `course-deck.txt`). Everything below comes from the deck unless it is marked **[Inference]** or **[Unclear]**. The deck has no images, charts, comments or hidden slides. Only slides 1 and 6–13 have speaker notes.

## Course identity
- **Code / name:** EEC 399, Community Outreach
- **Offered by:** Department of Electrical Engineering, IIT Roorkee, for third-year B.Tech students
- **Credits:** 2. They are "earned outside the classroom, in direct contact with people."
- **Instructors:** named only by the initials **"PP & SB"**, who are quoted on slide 2. **[Unclear]** The deck does not give their full names.
- **Batch:** 168 students. There are 8 fixed topics, teams of 5–7 members, and at most 4 teams per topic.
- Slide 1 credits "Fable 5" with doing some repetitive tasks on the deck. This has no bearing on the course.

## Course aims (slide 2)
- "An inward journey, not an outward one." The course "is not about simply giving back to society; it is more about understanding the challenges in our surroundings and asking the right questions for ourselves." The speaker note on slide 1 puts it as: "this is not charity, it is enquiry."
- The deck sets three principles:
  1. **Field work, not literature review.**
  2. **Identify, then engage:** recognise a societal problem around you and work with the people living it.
  3. **Ask whether technology helps.** "Sometimes the honest answer is no. That finding is also a result."
- Every team works on a community that already lives inside or beside the campus. None of the topics requires leaving Roorkee.
- Closing line (slide 22): "Ask the question you would not have asked otherwise."

## Rules
- **Rule 1, teams:** 5–7 members. An earlier document said 6–10; this deck replaces it.
- **Rule 2, topics:** one of eight is assigned by a public random draw. Topics are no longer open-ended.
- **Rule 3, relative grading:** a team is compared with the other teams holding the same topic (up to 4) and with the whole batch.

### Topic 3: Campus dogs, "Two sides of one street" (slide 8)
- Example question: "What happens when it's not safe to walk in a campus?"
- Suggested angles:
  - **Population mapping:** estimate numbers and territories across campus zones.
  - **The feeders' view:** who feeds, why, and what they have seen over the years.
  - **The afraid side:** bite incidents, near-misses, and routes people avoid after dark.
  - **Facts on record:** bite and vaccination figures at the health centre, sterilisation (ABC) status, and existing policy.
  - **A workable proposal:** "A coexistence plan both sides would actually sign."
- Speaker note: up to 4 teams may hold this topic, and they are graded against one another.

## Required deliverables and formats
### KPI table (slide 16, required for every team)
Each team sets five KPIs, one per category. The categories are fixed; the team sets its own targets.

| Category | What it measures | Example target | Example evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach | How many people you actually engaged | 25 individuals from the community | Consent sheet + contact log |
| Depth | Time spent in genuine contact | 40 person-hours of field time | Dated field diary, mentor-initialled |
| Record | A dataset or artefact that did not exist before | 1 mapped dataset of 6 gates / 40 stalls | Spreadsheet + methodology note |
| Output | Something handed back to the community or institute | 1 written submission to the concerned office | Acknowledged copy |
| Change | One concrete thing that shifted | 1 stated commitment or corrected practice | Written or recorded confirmation |

Slide 17 gives a worked example for Guards: 25 guards across at least 6 gates and 2 shifts; 40 person-hours including 3 night-shift visits, with a diary countersigned by guards; an anonymised exit-and-tenure table with a method note; a 2-page findings note to the Security Office with a dated acknowledgement; and one request raised and answered in writing. The deck says this shape is "just for guidance."

## Evaluation and grading
- **Weights:** **[Unclear]** The deck gives **no numeric weights** for the poster, KPI table, evidence or viva, and no mark split between group and individual work.
- **Method:** relative grading within the topic cluster and across the batch.
- **What separates the top team (slide 15):**
  - More primary contact: people actually spoken to, on record.
  - Evidence a sceptic would accept, not photographs of the team.
  - A question the other teams did not think of.
  - Honesty about what failed and what could not be verified.
- **Duplication:** some overlap between teams on a topic is expected, but "duplicate stuff will lead to deductions for all teams."
- **Where teams lost marks last year (slide 21):**
  - A one-day visit with no return visit.
  - A "team selfie" poster. The poster is about the community.
  - Untestable objectives such as "spread awareness."
  - Last-week evidence, such as consent sheets signed in bulk with dates that give it away.
  - Four identical reports from one topic cluster.
  - A team not on the same page, with members saying different things in their individual vivas.
- **Rubric:** **[Unclear]** There is no formal rubric. The criteria above are the closest the deck comes to one.

## Ethics, consent and documentation (slide 20, "Non-negotiable")
**Do:**
- Ask permission before recording, in the language the person speaks.
- Anonymise by default: use role and gate number, not names.
- Tell people what the work is for and that they may decline.
- Share what you found with the people you found it from.

**Do not:**
- Use any photograph or quote that could identify a person to their employer.
- Promise a job, money, or a fix you cannot deliver.
- Record children, or record identifiable household details of anyone's family.
- Name individual contractors or officials in the poster. The deck says to "report stuff, but be innovative."

The deck's reasoning: "A careless report can cost someone a job."

**Documentation requirements:** geotagged photographs, dated videos, dated consent sheets collected over time, a dated field diary, contact logs, and acknowledged copies of submissions.

## What this means for the campus dogs project
**[Inference]** None of this section is stated in the deck. It is my reading of how the deck's criteria apply to this project.

1. **Cover both sides of the street.** The topic is framed as two sides: feeders and volunteers, and the people who are afraid. Interview both, plus the neutral groups (security, mess workers, sanitation staff, health-centre staff), and keep a dated, anonymised contact log by role (for example "Mess worker, Bhawan X"). That log is your Reach evidence.
2. **Depth means repeat visits.** Visit at different times of day, including after dark, since the deck names "routes avoided after dark." Keep a dated field diary and get it initialled by whoever is the mentor. Ask who that is.
3. **Record:** build a geotagged dog population and territory map by campus zone, and a bite and near-miss hotspot map. Add a methodology note explaining how you avoided double-counting dogs. A dataset like this did not exist before, and it is hard for another team to copy.
4. **Facts on record:** request bite and vaccination figures from the institute health centre, and ask about ABC sterilisation status and existing policy. Put the request in writing. If you are refused or get no answer, report that honestly; the deck rewards admitting what could not be verified.
5. **Output and Change:** submit a 2-page coexistence proposal to the relevant office and keep the acknowledged copy. Possible offices are the Estate Office, Dean of Students, the health centre and Security. Aim for one written commitment, such as designated feeding zones, a vaccination or ABC drive, or better lighting on avoided routes. Sign-off from both feeders and fearful respondents would match "a plan both sides would actually sign."
6. **Ask whether technology helps.** Answer this explicitly, even if the answer is no. A bite-reporting form or a feeding-spot map are possible examples.
7. **Differentiate from the up to 3 other dog teams.** Find one question they are unlikely to ask, for example how mess food waste drives dog clustering, or how night-shift guards experience dogs. Duplicated surface facts cost marks for every team on the topic.
8. **Ethics:** anonymise everyone. Don't identify any mess or security worker, especially when they criticise policy. Don't name officials or contractors on the poster. Don't record children, for example faculty kids near the residential areas. Promise nothing.
9. **Evidence starts now.** Use geotagged photos of locations, not of the team, and dated audio with recorded consent. Store everything centrally as you collect it. Final review is "October sometime," so the deadline is close.
10. **Viva readiness:** every member should know the whole story and the numbers. Keep one shared fact sheet so that individual answers match.
